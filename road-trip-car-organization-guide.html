Car organization guide

Set up your trunk and backseat so it doesn't fall apart by day two.

Most trunk chaos isn't caused by having too much stuff. It's caused by having no assigned spot for anything. Here's how to zone your car once, at the start, so it stays usable for the whole trip.

1. Split your trunk into three zones, not one pile

Treat the trunk as three separate areas instead of one shared space everything gets thrown into:

  • Base layer: things you won't touch until you arrive: extra clothes, a cooler restock, camping gear.
  • Access layer: things you'll need at every stop: cooler, trash bag, shoes, a jacket.
  • Overflow: soft bags and loose items that shift; these go last, wedged around the edges so they can't slide during braking.

Load in that order: base layer first, access layer last, so you never have to unpack the whole trunk to reach a snack.

2. Keep the backseat map-shaped, not floor-shaped

Anything that ends up on the floor gets forgotten or kicked out of reach. A seat-back or seat-gap organizer keeps small items (chargers, snacks, wipes) visible and within arm's reach without competing with legroom.

3. Separate "wet" from "everything else" before you leave

Beach trips, rainy stops, and spilled drinks are the number one reason a clean trunk turns into a mess by hour three. One waterproof liner or dry bag, used consistently, keeps damp items from touching dry gear at all.

4. Match the system to your vehicle

🚗
Compact car or sedanUse slim, vertical, collapsible storage. Keep the most-used bag nearest the trunk opening and avoid rigid bins.
🚙
SUV or minivanCreate passenger, food, and overnight zones. Use tie-down points so the extra volume does not become shifting clutter.
🛻
Pickup truckPrioritize weatherproof, lockable containment and secure every item against movement in the bed.

5. Re-zone at every overnight stop, not just at the start

A trunk that's organized on day one and never touched again always ends up a mess by day three. Spend two minutes each evening returning items to their zones and moving tomorrow's first-use gear to the access layer.
